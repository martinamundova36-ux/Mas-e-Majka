Cupping: what should you expect?

Cupping involves placing cups on the skin to create suction.

How does it work?

The cups produce a vacuum that gently draws the skin upward. Temporary circular marks, redness and tenderness can occur after treatment.

What does research say?

Research on cupping for pain is limited in quality. Some studies suggest possible short-term benefits, but stronger evidence is needed. It is not a substitute for medical diagnosis or treatment.

Safety

Discuss your health history with your practitioner. Cupping may be unsuitable for some skin conditions, bleeding disorders and other medical circumstances.

Further reading: NIH NCCIH – Cupping.
